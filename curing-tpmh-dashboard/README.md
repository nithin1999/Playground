## Latest improvement — Pilot Freeze + Minimum Viable Observation
The project has enough analytical framework for the first pilot. Adding more rules immediately before Thursday risks confusing execution and contaminating the trial. Freeze the operating method before the pilot and collect a small set of decision-critical observations consistently.

### Freeze before Thursday
1. Name Op1, Op2, floor leader and qualified rescue/shadow roles.
2. Freeze Op1/Op2 zones/routes and critical protected work.
3. Verify demonstrated qualifications for critical tasks; unresolved gaps remain visible.
4. Freeze break/meal relief ownership and handoff.
5. Freeze NORMAL/ABNORMAL rule: one named abnormality owner; other operator protects due work unless approved cross-support is required.
6. Freeze rescue/containment triggers using existing plant safety, quality and escalation rules. Do not invent new safety limits for the study.
7. Brief the crew and leader from one common instruction sheet. Wednesday is the method-freeze point; Thursday is execution/observation, not live redesign.

## Minimum viable pilot record
For each meaningful exception capture: `time | Op1 activity | Op2 activity | abnormality | owner | response start | extra helper? | helper start/end | displaced/delayed work | recovery complete | note`.

Also record pilot context: actual staffing, running presses/major downtime, production plan/actual, breaks/relief, unusual maintenance or special conditions.

### Primary pilot question
**How often, why, and for how many people-minutes did the two-operator system require staffing-substitution help?**

Third-person production help is not hidden: record it as support people-minutes. Coaching/observation is distinguished from hands-on production substitution.

## Thursday decision interpretation
- **Encouraging:** required work is completed, breaks are covered, production remains controlled, and little/no staffing-substitution help is required.
- **Redesign evidence:** recurring help, delayed protected work, relief failure, repeated cross-zone overload, persistent backlog, or substantial recovery burden.
- **Contain/stop:** follow existing plant safety/quality/process authority whenever control is compromised.

Do not invent numerical acceptance limits. Use existing plant requirements and observed baseline/pilot evidence.

## Risks / gaps
- Operators may behave differently because they know the study is occurring; leader behavior must be consistent and non-punitive.
- A qualified third person can unintentionally make a weak design look successful through reminders, fetching, prioritizing or brief unlogged help.
- Thursday may be an unusually easy or difficult production condition; retain the Exposure Signature.
- Break coverage can export work to Mold/Bladder/Team Lead; record resulting home-role delay/recovery rather than treating relief as free.
- Fixed zones can create local overload; do not improvise frequent zone switching during the pilot unless the frozen abnormal rule calls for it.
- Tricycle rejection means residual walking remains part of the staffing design.
