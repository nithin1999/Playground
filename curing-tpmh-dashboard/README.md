## Latest improvement — two-mode dispatch standard
Do not force the future 2-operator Curing job into one rigid repeating cycle. Machine-intensive work is partly event-driven: alarms and equipment conditions change where the operator must go. Freeze two linked standards instead.

**NORMAL mode:** each operator follows the workload/travel-balanced geographic route for predictable GIP/scan, checks, monitoring, material/document tasks and other protected routine work.

**ABNORMAL mode:** an alarm or defined abnormality temporarily interrupts the normal route and activates a visible dispatch priority ladder. The zone owner responds first when qualified/available; the other operator cross-supports under the response matrix; if both are occupied, required qualification is unavailable, or a plant-defined safety/quality/process condition is reached, escalate under the Green/Amber/Red matrix. Any interrupted routine work becomes visible pending work with owner and recovery status rather than disappearing from the workload.

After the abnormality is controlled, return to NORMAL mode only after the operator checks the visible pending-work queue and restores protected work in plant-required priority order. Do not invent numeric queue/WIP limits; use existing required deadlines where available and establish operational limits from baseline/shadow evidence.

This creates a more realistic future-state standard for machine-intensive Curing: a stable routine route plus a standardized dispatch/recovery method for stochastic work.

## Pilot gates
- **Gate 0 — Data/design:** mapped layout, normalized alarm evidence, travel study and workload/travel-balanced candidate zones.
- **Gate 1 — Standard work/skills:** NORMAL route, ABNORMAL dispatch/recovery standard, routine-work matrix, response matrix, visible pending-work queue and verified critical-task coverage.
- **Gate 2 — Relief/capacity:** Relief Capacity Reservation Matrix plus preliminary 30-minute capacity profile for normal, abnormal and break windows.
- **Gate 3 — Management/measurement:** measurement dictionary, observer check, Leader Standard Work, visual controls, SIC ownership, intervention taxonomy, and Pilot Escalation & Recovery Matrix.
- **Gate 4 — Baseline/context:** current 3-op state measured with identical definitions, including actual relief performance and pending-work recovery.
- **Gate 5 — Shadow 2-op:** capture busy/free state, response labor, interventions, relief exceptions, displaced work, recovery aging, route exceptions and escalation triggers.
- **Gate 6 — Revise:** Pareto causes and revise the operating system.
- **Gate 7 — Sustained trial:** full crew rotation without hidden support, recurring overload, missed protected work, failed relief or persistent carryover.
- **Gate 8 — True 2-op/control:** demonstrated acceptance across representative conditions; controlled standard work, skills, relief, escalation, SIC and control plan.
