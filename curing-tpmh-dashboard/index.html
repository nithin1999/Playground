Latest improvement — travel burden & zone robustness

The two-zone design must not be frozen from press count or average workload alone. In machine-intensive Curing, some movement is signal-driven and therefore consumes operator capacity. The next design proof is a Travel Burden / Motion Loss study.

Observe: origin → destination → reason → time/distance for representative operator trips.
Classify: required abnormal-response travel, required routine route, or avoidable fetch/search/document/material travel.
Redesign: use a spaghetti overlay + from-to frequency matrix to move feasible items to point of use and compare candidate zone boundaries.
Normalize: walking minutes/distance per 100 running press-hours and avoidable fetch/search trips per shift.

Do not classify all walking as waste: alarm-response travel can be necessary. No travel reduction is claimed until the future-state layout is physically tested and measured.

Best current strategy

  1. Map presses + actual travel and classify required vs avoidable motion.
  2. Balance two zones by occupied workload, peaks/concurrency and travel.
  3. Implement feasible point-of-use changes and define future-state routes.
  4. Protect standardized routine work; use signal-driven abnormal response.
  5. Verify two-deep skills and break-relief coverage.
  6. Include measured travel in 30-minute capacity-margin proof.
  7. Freeze measurement/Leader Standard Work/SIC definitions.
  8. Collect context-characterized 3-op baseline with identical dependency, backlog and travel measures.
  9. Shadow 2-op; Pareto overload, support, backlog and routing/travel losses.
  10. Revise → sustained A/B/C/D rotation → true 2-op only after gates pass.

Next action

Run a representative travel-burden observation. Overlay the paths on the floor map, create a from-to frequency matrix, then propose two candidate zone layouts and point-of-use changes. Compare them on measured occupied work + peak demand + travel rather than equal press count.
