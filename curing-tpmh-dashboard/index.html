Latest improvement — Visible Abnormality Queue & Dispatch

The response clock now needs a visible dispatch rule for overlapping abnormalities. Two operators need to see what is waiting, who owns it and what must be handled first without hiding queue growth.

VISIBLE: every active event shows press, signal time, approved condition/priority class, owner and state.
PRIORITY: approved safety/quality/containment criticality overrides ordinary sequence; do not invent thresholds.
FIFO WITHIN EQUAL PRIORITY: normally take the oldest equivalent-priority call next so work is not buried or cherry-picked.
OWN: one operator owns each event; the other follows the normal route unless dispatched or a qualified two-person response is required.
AGE / ESCALATE: make waiting age and escalation state visible; use approved plant reaction rules if capacity is exceeded.
RECOVER: keep displaced protected work and relief debt visible until recovered.

Best current strategy

  1. Remove avoidable motion and point-of-use gaps.
  2. Demand-weight Op1/Op2 zones by work + travel + abnormal exposure.
  3. Build individual capacity envelopes and collision reserve.
  4. Prove skill readiness and time-phased qualified relief.
  5. Freeze normal routes, protected-work priorities and workload states.
  6. Use signal → acknowledge → arrival → hands-on → containment → recovery timestamps.
  7. Use a visible abnormality queue: approved criticality first, FIFO within equal priority, named owner, waiting age and escalation state.
  8. Account for relief debt and home-role recovery labor.
  9. Collect context-characterized 3-op baseline and Exposure Signatures.
  10. Freeze GO / HOLD / STOP criteria and shadow rescue rules.
  11. Run shadow 2-op with rescue-only third operator; log every intervention and dispatch decision.
  12. Compare exposure-matched windows using TPMH, Effective Labor, Support Burden, Adjusted TPMH and response/queue distributions.
  13. Pareto exceptions, redesign and repeat across representative A/B/C/D conditions before true 2-op.
