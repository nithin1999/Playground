Latest improvement — two-deep skills coverage

The staffing model now requires task-level qualification coverage, not just a named backup. A two-operator design can fail when the only qualified responder for a critical task is on break, already committed, or protecting Mold/Bladder due work.

Normal: both proposed Curing operators are qualified for routine zone work and the common abnormalities they are expected to cross-support.
Critical tasks: verify a primary and alternate for safety/quality-critical, production-stopping, manual-recovery and relief tasks.
Relief: qualification coverage must remain available during break/lunch and backup-role due-work windows.
Training gap: a single-qualified critical task is a pre-pilot gap; close it with documented Job Instruction and demonstrated competence before counting the backup.

No inferred competence: mark qualification only from verified evidence. Current qualification adequacy is unknown until the matrix is supplied.

Best current strategy

  1. Map presses; balance zones by occupied workload, peaks and travel.
  2. Standardize predictable work; keep abnormalities signal-driven.
  3. Freeze abnormal-response ownership, cross-support and escalation.
  4. Verify two-deep task coverage across operators, relief windows and crews.
  5. Prove break relief and 30-minute capacity margin.
  6. Freeze measurement definitions, escalation authority and leader standard work.
  7. Collect context-characterized 3-op baseline with SIC.
  8. Shadow 2-op: measure concurrent demand, qualification/availability failures and all interventions.
  9. Pareto → revise → sustained A/B/C/D rotation → true 2-op only after gates pass.
