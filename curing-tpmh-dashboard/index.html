Latest improvement — Time-of-Shift Robustness

Do not let the 12-hour average hide deterioration. Preserve timestamps and compare early, middle and late shift behavior under comparable demand.

A late-shift worsening under comparable exposure is a robustness concern to investigate, not automatic proof of fatigue. No arbitrary pass/fail threshold has been invented.

Qualified capacity diagnosis

Both qualified operators committed + queue: physical capacity collision.
Operator free but not qualified + queue: skill-constrained capacity.
Qualified operator free + queue: investigate ownership, visibility, routing or priority.
Repeated third-person help: distinguish normal support from 2-op staffing substitution.

Thursday observation

Continuous: abnormalities, queues, flex assists, outside help, exposure-state changes, breaks, delayed work, containment and recovery.

Fixed 15 min/hour: work category, walking/retrieval, waiting/monitoring, machine interaction, zone adherence, overlapping demand, relief and carryover. These samples are diagnostic, not full-shift utilization.

Break relief + flex

Breaks: COVERED / DEGRADED / UNRELIEVED-CONTAINED; keep relief debt visible until recovered.

Flex: CALL → CHECK → TRANSFER → WORK → RETURN → RECOVER. Repeated one-way borrowing signals a rebalance opportunity.

Best current strategy

  1. Freeze people, zones, routes, qualifications, relief and reaction rules.
  2. Scenario-walk simultaneous demand, break abnormality, flex assist and two-person tasks.
  3. Run Thursday with continuous exception logging, fixed observation windows and hourly production control.
  4. Reconstruct Op1/Op2 occupancy and overlay queues, breaks and support.
  5. Diagnose physical-capacity vs skill/ownership/routing failures.
  6. Normalize work against running presses, events, mix and downtime.
  7. Compare early/middle/late shift robustness.
  8. Review TPMH, Effective Labor, Independent Exposure, support, relief debt and queue/recovery.
  9. Pareto observed causes; cross-train/rebalance only where evidence supports it.
  10. Repeat representative conditions before permanent staffing change.
