Latest improvement — Cross-Zone Collision Matrix

Average workload and window-level reserve can still miss a critical 2-op failure mode: simultaneous demand can couple the two operators, especially when abnormalities occur in both zones or both need the same scarce qualified support.

Observed, not assumed: assign every timestamped abnormal event to the candidate Op1/Op2 zone and examine observed cross-zone overlaps. Do not assume independent alarm arrivals.
Operational collision: distinguish same-zone vs cross-zone overlap, one-person vs two-person work, and competition for Team Lead/Mold/Bladder/relief resources.
Screening limitation: same-minute alarm starts show concurrency exists but do not prove simultaneous hands-on labor. Actual response intervals and operator state are still required.
Design use: test alternate zone boundaries against both workload balance and collision exposure before freezing the first-pilot layout.

Best current strategy

  1. Remove avoidable motion and point-of-use gaps.
  2. Build Demand-Weighted Operator Balance; define provisional zones by work content + travel + abnormal exposure.
  3. Build fixed-window Variability-Aware Reserve distributions for Op1, Op2 and the pair.
  4. Build Cross-Zone Collision Matrix and test alternate zone boundaries.
  5. Freeze NORMAL routes and ABNORMAL single-owner dispatch.
  6. Protect due work with the Protected Work Matrix and visible recovery queue.
  7. Verify actual Op1/Op2/relief skills, including two-person/scarce-skill tasks.
  8. Use formal break handoffs and retain relief person's home-role demand.
  9. Freeze measurement, escalation and containment/restart rules.
  10. Collect context-characterized 3-op baseline with reserve + collision evidence.
  11. Shadow 2-op; measure queue aging, support labor, breaks, containment and collision handling.
  12. Calculate scheduled TPMH, Effective Labor and Adjusted TPMH.
  13. Pareto exceptions; rebalance work/zone boundaries and repeat.
  14. Expand across demand, downtime, late-shift and A/B/C/D conditions before true 2-op.
