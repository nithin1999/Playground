Latest improvement — abnormal-response ownership & escalation

The two-operator design now requires a one-page decision matrix for abnormal work. Alarm frequency alone is insufficient: the key feasibility question is whether a new abnormality arrives while one or both operators are already occupied, and whether the system can route and recover without hidden labor or backlog.

Normal: Op 1 and Op 2 own abnormalities in their workload-balanced geographic zones.
Cross-support: if the zone owner is already committed, the other qualified Curing operator responds when their own zone condition permits.
Escalate: if both operators are committed, the work exceeds qualified scope, or plant safety/quality/production rules require it, summon the named qualified backup/leader and log provider + reason + minutes.
Recover: return to zone ownership after containment and make displaced routine work/carryover visible.

No invented response-time limit: derive limits from plant requirements and measured notification, arrival, hands-on and recovery evidence.

Best current strategy

  1. Map presses; balance zones by occupied workload, peaks and travel.
  2. Standardize predictable work; keep automatic-machine abnormalities signal-driven.
  3. Freeze abnormal-response ownership, cross-support and escalation rules.
  4. Prove break relief and 30-minute capacity margin.
  5. Freeze measurement definitions, escalation authority and leader standard work.
  6. Collect context-characterized 3-op baseline with SIC/time-of-shift fields.
  7. Shadow 2-op: measure concurrent occupied-response demand and every routing override/intervention.
  8. Pareto → revise → sustained A/B/C/D rotation → true 2-op only after all gates pass.

Next action

Draft the Abnormal-Response Ownership & Escalation Matrix. For the next representative observation/shadow window add five fields: Op 1 free/busy, Op 2 free/busy, responder, escalation/backup used, and displaced-work/carryover. This converts alarm counts into an actual coverage/queue test.
