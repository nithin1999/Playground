Latest improvement: add Leader Standard Work and named escalation ownership to the pilot. The two-operator system must prove it works without hidden Team Lead, Mold/Bladder, supervisor or third-operator labor.

Pilot Leader Standard Work

  1. Start-of-shift readiness: qualifications, planned production, known equipment constraints, Mold/Bladder due work and relief coverage.
  2. Visual status review: plan vs actual, zone status, abnormalities, intervention minutes and support backlog.
  3. Named escalation owner: define who receives the call and who can restore third-operator/extra coverage.
  4. Respond to abnormalities: restore required coverage first; record cause/provider/minutes rather than allowing hidden support.
  5. End-of-shift reflection: review intervention Pareto, assign countermeasure/owner and decide proceed vs revise.

Why this changes the design

Operator SW + Leader SW
Operator standard defines how two people run Curing. Leader standard defines how management supports the standard and responds when actual conditions differ.
No free backup capacity
Every Team Lead, Mold, Bladder, supervisor or third-operator support event is logged by provider and minutes.
Protect the conclusion
A pilot that reaches production only by quietly borrowing labor does not demonstrate a sustainable manpower reduction.

Best current strategy

  1. Map press layout and make alarm source visible.
  2. Balance Op 1 / Op 2 by occupied workload, peaks and travel.
  3. Create two-operator standardized work and task-qualified backup.
  4. Create Pilot Acceptance Matrix + Leader Standard Work.
  5. Use point-of-use staging to reduce routine travel.
  6. Protect Mold/Bladder due work and carryover.
  7. Baseline → shadow 2-op → Pareto/revise → sustained rotation → true 2-op.
