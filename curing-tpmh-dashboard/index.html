Latest improvement — Variability-Aware Workload Reserve

Do not validate two operators from average utilization alone. Preserve the distribution of fixed-window operator demand so short overloads, repeated overloads and recovery behavior remain visible.

Reserve: available qualified operator minutes − observed due workload minutes, calculated for Op1, Op2 and the pair in each fixed window.
Tail behavior: track negative-reserve windows, consecutive negative windows, queue growth/recovery and outside-support people-minutes rather than hiding them inside a shift average.
Context match: compare windows with similar running press-hours/cycles, production mix, downtime, break state and time-on-shift.
No invented threshold: do not declare an acceptable percentile or overload rate until baseline/pilot evidence and plant acceptance criteria support one. Alarm duration remains excluded as labor.

Best current strategy

  1. Remove avoidable motion and point-of-use gaps.
  2. Build Demand-Weighted Operator Balance; define provisional zones by work content + travel + abnormal exposure.
  3. Build fixed-window Variability-Aware Reserve distributions for Op1, Op2 and the pair.
  4. Freeze NORMAL routes and ABNORMAL single-owner dispatch.
  5. Protect due work with the Protected Work Matrix and visible recovery queue.
  6. Verify actual Op1/Op2/relief skills and formal break handoffs.
  7. Freeze measurement, escalation and containment/restart rules.
  8. Collect context-characterized 3-op baseline with workload/reserve distributions.
  9. Shadow 2-op; measure queue aging, support labor, breaks, containment and tail-window overload.
  10. Calculate scheduled TPMH, Effective Labor and Adjusted TPMH.
  11. Pareto exceptions; rebalance work/zone boundaries and repeat.
  12. Expand across demand, downtime, late-shift and A/B/C/D conditions before true 2-op.
