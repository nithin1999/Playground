Latest improvement — Time-on-Shift Exposure Control

A 12-hour pilot can look feasible early in the shift while accumulated queue demand, delayed relief or later-shift workload exposes a weakness. Pilot evidence is therefore now stratified by hours since shift start instead of treating every observation as exchangeable.

Tag: shift start, hours since start, planned/actual break and relief person on every observation/event.
Compare: Capacity Reserve, queue age/recovery, abnormal demand, outside-support people-minutes and containment events by time on shift.
Protect: actual relief is part of the staffing design; delayed or interrupted relief is recorded rather than hidden inside a shift average.
Validate: early-shift success alone is not representative proof. Deliberately cover later-shift and break conditions before recommending true 2-op.

No universal fatigue or pass/fail threshold is invented; use plant requirements and measured baseline/pilot evidence.

Best current strategy

  1. Remove avoidable motion and balance zones on workload + peaks + travel.
  2. Freeze NORMAL routes and ABNORMAL dispatch with one owner per event.
  3. Protect displaced work in the visible recovery queue.
  4. Verify actual Op1/Op2/relief skill coverage.
  5. Reserve real break-relief capacity.
  6. Build qualification-constrained 30-minute Capacity Reserve + Recovery Profiles.
  7. Freeze measurement, escalation, SIC and containment/restart rules.
  8. Add time-on-shift and actual relief timing to the Context Card.
  9. Collect context-characterized 3-op baseline with the same definitions.
  10. Shadow 2-op and capture exceptions, queue/recovery, support labor, breaks and containment.
  11. Calculate scheduled TPMH, Effective Labor and Adjusted TPMH.
  12. Compare matched 3-op/2-op periods and stratify by time on shift.
  13. Pareto recurring overload/support/backlog/relief causes; redesign.
  14. Expand across late-shift, break, demand, downtime and A/B/C/D conditions before true 2-op.
