Latest improvement — Operator-Minute Capacity Envelope

Two operators over a 12-hour shift provide 1,440 scheduled operator-minutes, but scheduled minutes are not the same as discretionary abnormal-response capacity. Build an interval-level capacity ledger from observed/valid standard work before judging whether 2-op has enough reserve.

Capacity ledger: scheduled operator-minutes − recurring manual work − required travel − protected/fixed work − planned unavailable time = pre-abnormal reserve.
Then charge: observed abnormal hands-on work + two-person/collision work + recovery work. Keep outside-support people-minutes visible and include them in Effective Labor.
Rule: alarm duration is not hands-on labor. Do not invent a utilization/reserve target; establish distributions from baseline and shadow observations.

Best current strategy

  1. Remove avoidable motion and point-of-use gaps.
  2. Demand-weight Op1/Op2 zones by work + travel + abnormal exposure.
  3. Build interval Operator-Minute Capacity Envelope and usable reserve.
  4. Test variability, cross-zone collisions and shared resources.
  5. Apply qualification redundancy / pair robustness.
  6. Freeze NORMAL routes and single-owner ABNORMAL dispatch.
  7. Use visual ownership plus NORMAL → CONSTRAINED → SATURATED → RECOVERY states.
  8. Protect due work with Protected Work Matrix + Due-Time Recovery Queue.
  9. Use qualified break relief and account for relief debt/home-role recovery.
  10. Use leader standard work, process confirmation, scenario challenge and readiness gate.
  11. Collect matched 3-op baseline with interval capacity ledger.
  12. Run controlled shadow 2-op with rescue-only third operator.
  13. Calculate TPMH, Effective Labor, Support Burden and Adjusted TPMH.
  14. Compare stable-condition reserve, saturation, collisions, support, relief debt and recovery.
  15. Apply Time-of-Shift Robustness Gate across the 12-hour operating window.
  16. Pareto exceptions, redesign and repeat across representative A/B/C/D conditions before true 2-op.
