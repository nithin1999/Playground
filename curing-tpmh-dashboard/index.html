Latest improvement — Workload-State Escalation Ladder

Alarm count alone does not measure usable operator reserve. One intervention can consume both operators while several notifications may require little hands-on labor. Control the 2-op system by actual operator commitment, qualification, protected work and recovery demand.

NORMAL: both operators can execute assigned work and protected work remains controlled.
CONSTRAINED: one operator is committed to abnormal work, relief or protected work; the other follows the defined priority route and new demand is explicitly owned or queued.
SATURATED: both operators are committed, or the only available operator is not qualified, while additional required work arrives or due work becomes at risk. Apply existing plant escalation/containment and log support labor.
RECOVERY: abnormality is contained but deferred work remains. Recover by plant priority/required-by rules until the queue is controlled.

Do not invent alarm-count, response-time or utilization thresholds. Timestamp transitions during baseline/shadow observations and later quantify time in state, SATURATED entries, support labor, recovery time and carryover.
